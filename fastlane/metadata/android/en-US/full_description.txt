Mode Finder is a reference for guitar players: choose a root and a mode, and the
neck shows every note that belongs to it, with the root called out.

It is meant to be looked at while playing. The fretboard runs from the open
string to fret 24, with double dots at 12 and 24, so nothing is cut off. Notes
outside the scale are dimmed to near-black rather than removed, so the shape of
the mode stands out against the rest of the neck.

Three families of modes are included: the modes of the major scale, of the
harmonic minor and of the melodic minor. Spellings are worked out, not typed in:
every note is found by walking the letters and choosing the accidentals that
need the fewest signs, so A Aeolian reads as A B C D E F G rather than as a row
of sharps.

Variants are the other half of the idea. Drop two of the seven degrees and see
which five-note shape is left: minor pentatonic, major pentatonic, Hirajoshi,
In-sen, Man Gong, Egyptian and the rest. The two notes that were dropped stay on
the neck, dimmed and struck through, so the difference is visible rather than
implied.

The app is a single HTML file with everything inside it: about 29 KB, no
network, no permissions, no analytics, no ads. It never asks for the INTERNET
permission, so it could not reach anywhere even if it tried. It works in
aeroplane mode, in a basement, forever.

The theory is computed rather than stored as tables: the parent scale by
rotating the mode's step pattern, the note spellings by a letter walk with the
fewest accidentals, and the variants by masking two degrees and looking the
resulting pitch-class set up in a shape table. If the arithmetic were wrong
anywhere, it would be wrong visibly.

Source code, the browser version and the build script are all in the repository.
The build is reproducible: the same sources and the same toolchain produce the
same bytes, and every release lists the md5 and sha256 of the APK.
